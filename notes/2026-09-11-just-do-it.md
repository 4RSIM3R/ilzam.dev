the thing that actually triggered this: a project that is, honestly, 5 screens of CRUD and 1 screen that reads a materialized view. that's it. that's the whole scope. and yet getting it done takes tickets, story points, sprint ceremony, design review, multiple environments, a pipeline, sign-offs, before a single line of code gets written. the actual coding part, the part that produces the thing anyone will ever use, is maybe a few days of work buried inside weeks of process.

so the question i keep asking myself: is software engineering as a job partly invented? not the coding, the coding is real. but the layer on top, the methodology talk, the ceremony, the process for the sake of process, does that scale with how hard the problem actually is, or does it scale with something else entirely, org anxiety, headcount justification, people needing to look busy, career ladders that reward "process ownership" over shipped work?

because sqlite and linux didn't come from someone writing an RFC about their SDLC. they came from someone with a real technical problem, solving it directly, in public, and iterating. the jargon came after, from people writing books and conference talks about how they did it. and now we hire the jargon before we've earned the right to have any.

if a task is genuinely 5 CRUD screens, the honest move is to just write the 5 screens. not invent a ceremony to make it feel like more.
